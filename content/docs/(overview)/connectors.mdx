Much of your data starts somewhere else: tasks in Todoist, issues on GitHub, articles in an RSS feed. **A connector is a program that reads such a service, its vendor, and writes what it finds to your instance as items.** It can also carry changes made in Marfa back to the vendor.

## A program you run

**The server never runs a connector and never calls a vendor.** A connector is an ordinary program that you run wherever suits you, on a schedule or all the time, and it reaches the instance through the same API as any app. That keeps the server small, and your vendor credentials out of it.

A connector runs with a key made for it alone. The key's source labels everything the connector writes, and together with each record's ID at the vendor, it lets the connector find a record again and update it rather than write it twice.

What a connector brings in belongs in the **feed**, which holds records as they arrived, until a person or an app keeps one in the library. A connector names `feed` on each write, or its key makes `feed` its default; otherwise its records land in the library. [Items and types](/items-and-types#library-and-feed) explains the two tiers.

## What the instance keeps for a connector

A connector can stop, restart or move to another computer without losing its place, because the instance keeps what it needs:

| What | What it's for |
| --- | --- |
| A registration | Names the connector, and shows its last heartbeat and how its recent runs went. |
| A state document | A JSON object the connector resumes from, such as the vendor's sync token. |
| Current problems | Messages about unresolved trouble, such as one record the vendor refused, shown until the connector clears them. |
| Agreements | One record per item of what the connector and the vendor last agreed about it, so a two-way connector can tell a change a person made from its own. |
| A hold | Lets one process at a time write, so two copies of a connector started by mistake don't overwrite each other. |

**The state document, current problems and agreements belong to the key's source, not to one key.** Replace a connector's key with a new one under the same source, and the connector picks up where the old one stopped.

## Webhooks from the vendor

Many vendors post a webhook when something changes, so a connector can act at once instead of at its next scheduled read. The instance can give a connector an endpoint, a private address the vendor posts to.

**The instance stores each delivery as it arrived, and the connector does the rest**: it reads the deliveries, checks each came from the vendor, and marks them handled. [Receive vendor webhooks](/guides/connector-webhooks) shows how.
