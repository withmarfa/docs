Your notes, tasks, reading and files usually live inside the apps that made them, each in its own format. Marfa keeps that data in one place you own instead, in shapes every app understands. **Apps become ways to look at your data, not places that keep it.**

<Callout title="Before the first release">Marfa hasn't had its first release yet. These docs describe Marfa as it is on its `main` branch, and they change as it does.</Callout>

## What you can do with Marfa

- **Keep your data when you change apps.** Stop using an app, and everything it saved stays in Marfa for the next one.
- **Give AI agents your data.** An agent reads and writes your notes, tasks and files through the CLI or the API, with a key that reaches only the kinds of data you choose.
- **Build apps on data that's already there.** A reading list, a dashboard or a personal CRM starts full, because your other apps already saved what it shows.
- **Bring in data from services you use.** Connectors copy records from GitHub, Todoist or an RSS feed into Marfa, and some send changes back.
- **Work offline.** Apps keep a copy of your data on the device and send their changes when they can reach Marfa again.

If you know Apple's platforms, two comparisons help. Marfa is **like iCloud, but for structured data and open to any app**. And it's **like HealthKit, but for all your personal information**: each app asks for the kinds of data it needs, such as notes or tasks, and sees nothing else.

## How it fits together

One running Marfa is an **instance**. It belongs to one person, its **owner**, and runs on a computer they choose: their own Mac, a home server, or a host such as Railway. Everything else connects to it.

<MarfaAtAGlance />

- **Apps and scripts** read and write over Marfa's HTTP API, each with a credential that reaches only the data it was given.
- **AI agents** work the same way, often through the CLI, Marfa's command-line tool.
- **Devices**, such as an app on your iPhone, keep a working copy of the data they need. They work offline and send their changes later.
- **Connectors** bring in records from other services, and can carry changes back to them.

The instance is the source of truth. Every app, device and connector reads from it and writes to it, and the instance decides what happens when two of them change the same thing. [How Marfa works](/how-marfa-works) explains each part.

## What Marfa isn't

- **Not shared.** An instance belongs to one person. It holds that person's data, and other people don't collaborate in it.
- **Not hosted for you.** Your data lives on the machine you run Marfa on, and in any storage you choose to attach.
- **Not an app.** You don't need any particular app. Apps are built on Marfa, and anyone can build one.
- **Not a place to run code.** Apps, agents and connectors run outside Marfa and reach it over its API.

## Where it runs

Marfa keeps your data in one SQLite database file and a folder of files, so there's no database server to set up. Run it on your own computer, or deploy the ready-made template on Railway.

Your own devices reach it over your home network, or from anywhere over a private network such as Tailscale. To reach it from the internet, give it a public address on your own domain through a Cloudflare Tunnel. [Deploy](/guides/deploy) covers each.
