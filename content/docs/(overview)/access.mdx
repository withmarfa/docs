Your instance holds everything about you, so each **credential** reaches only what it was given. A notes app can read and write notes and nothing else.

## Who can act

Three kinds of caller act on an instance:

| Caller | How it acts | What it reaches |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The owner | Signs in to the instance in a browser | Managing the instance, listing item summaries, managing the bin, reading item counts and exporting data. |
| A key | Sent with each request, as `Authorization: Bearer marfa_k1_…` | What it was made with |
| An app | Signs in through OAuth, and sends its access token, `marfa_at_…` | What the owner approved |

Through a **browser session**, the owner manages the instance without a key, and sees each item's summary but **never its content**. [What the owner's browser reaches](/run/sign-in-as-the-owner#what-the-owners-browser-reaches) lists it. A credential sent beside the session alone decides the request's access, so no key or app ever gets the owner's authority.

The computer the instance runs on has an authority of its own. The CLI uses it through a [private socket](/run/deploy#the-private-socket), reachable only by the account running the server, to claim an instance, make the first key and recover the owner's password.

## What a credential reaches

A credential is limited in two ways. Its **maps** say which data it reads and writes, and its **permissions** say which other operations it may use.

A map names things at a level, `read` or `write`, and `write` includes reading. Anything a map doesn't name is out of reach. A key has five:

| Map | Field | What it names | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Type | `type_permissions` | Item types, by identifier or pattern | `"core.*": "write"` |
| Edge | `edge_permissions` | Edge types | `"about": "read"` |
| Extension | `extension_permissions` | Extension namespaces | `"com.example.reader": "write"` |
| Metadata | `metadata_permissions` | Registering types and edge types | `"types": "write"` |
| Profile | `profile_permissions` | The owner's name, email and picture | `"email": "read"` |

A few rules apply to single maps:

- **The type map** matters most. Only it takes a third level, `none`, which shuts out a type its wildcard covers.
- **The metadata map** covers the definitions on your instance, not an item's tags and extensions.
- **The extension map** is a key's alone: an app reaches no extensions.
- **The profile map** can write only the owner's name and picture, never the sign-in email.

## What a refusal reveals

An item of a type the credential **can't read** answers `404 item_not_found`, as an item that doesn't exist does, so a refusal reveals nothing. An edge from such an item, and a blob only such items name, answer as missing too, though a readable edge still names a hidden target's ID. An item the credential can read but not write is refused with `403 type_not_permitted`.

So a notes app's key that may write notes reads a task as missing, and a key that may only read tasks is refused when it changes one.

## Permissions

A permission opens a group of operations, such as reading the audit log or making keys. It opens no items: a key with every permission and no type map reads nothing, and every item operation is refused `403 type_not_permitted`.

The Catalog's [Permissions](/catalog/permissions) page lists them all.

## How a key or an app gets access

You make a key as the owner, or with a key or an app that holds the `keys.mint` permission, and say what it holds. A key made by another credential can hold only the permissions and maps its maker holds, and can't outlive its maker's expiry. [Manage keys](/run/manage-keys) shows how.

**Signing in by approval is the preferred way for an app to reach Marfa.** The app registers itself and sends the owner to an approval page, which can give it less than it asked for. The app holds only what the owner approved, until the owner narrows or disconnects it, or it goes unused for the inactivity period, a year by default. An AI agent that connects to the [MCP server](/marfa-mcp-server) signs in the same way. [Approve an app](/run/manage-apps#approve-an-app) shows the owner's side, and [Authenticate](/build/authenticate) the app's.

An app may also use a key, given by the owner or minted with `keys.mint`. A key an app mints stays within the app's grant and carries the app's `client_id`.
