---
title: What the CLI, connectors and apps send
description: See what the CLI and connectors report about themselves, when, how to turn it off, and what to expect of an app.
---

The CLI, connectors and apps report on themselves apart from the instance. **None sends anything until it's given a destination**: the CLI and a connector by the environment they run in, and an app by the people who build it. What an instance sends is in [Send an instance's telemetry](/run/telemetry).

## What the CLI sends

**The CLI sends nothing unless its environment names a destination.** `OTEL_SDK_DISABLED=true` turns off both reports:

| Report | Sent when | What it holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One trace for each command | `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_ENDPOINT` or `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_TRACES_ENDPOINT` is set | The command's name, its exit code, the core's operations beneath it with their counts and outcomes, and the error code of a refusal |
| One exception to PostHog | `MARFA_POSTHOG_HOST` and `MARFA_POSTHOG_PROJECT_TOKEN` are both set, and the command ends in a fault | The command's name, the error and exit codes, the version, the operating system and architecture, and a fixed sentence for the kind of error |

**Neither report holds an argument, address, credential, item content or message,** and neither names a person. As on the server, an exception needs no OTLP endpoint. A destination that is unreachable or slow changes neither the command's output nor its exit code, and the command waits for it no longer than its timeout, two seconds by default.

**A fault is something the CLI or the core got wrong,** such as a panic or an answer it can't read. A refusal, a usage error, Ctrl-C, being offline, a timeout, a rate limit and any `5xx`, which the server reports itself, send none.

A folder watch that [starts when you log in](/use/keep-folders-syncing#start-the-watch-when-you-log-in) carries the settings set in the shell that turned it on. [The CLI's telemetry reference](https://github.com/withmarfa/marfa/blob/main/core/marfa-cli/COMMANDS.md#telemetry) lists every setting it reads, and every kind of fault.

## What connectors send

**A connector built on the connector kit sends nothing of its own** unless its environment sets `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_ENDPOINT`. Then it exports its traces and logs over OTLP to that address, and nowhere else.

A failed run reaches you through your instance, not through telemetry: the connector reports the run with its error, and `marfa attention` lists a connector whose last run failed. A start that fails before the connector registers is only logged, and exported over OTLP when that is on. [Make a connector follow changes](/build/connector-follow-changes#read-a-failed-run) shows how to read a failed run.

## What apps send

**What an app sends is decided by the people who build it,** and its own settings say how to turn it off. An app built on the Swift SDK can report its errors, and log events it chooses, through the SDK, which sends nothing until the app gives it a destination and turns it on, and keeps item content, credentials and the instance's address out of every report. An app may also measure its own use, or capture crashes, with tools of its own. [Report errors](/reference/swift-sdk/errors-and-testing#report-errors) says what the SDK's reports hold.
