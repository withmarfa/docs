Marfa is one home, on your own Mac or server, for everything you keep: **your files and pictures, your notes and bookmarks, and what you keep in other services.** Every app and agent you approve works from that one copy, and reaches only what you allow. Use it from Claude or ChatGPT, from the command line, or as a folder on your Mac, and **keep your data when you change apps.**

<Callout type="warn" title="Marfa is early">Expect breaking changes until version 1.0. The [Changelog](/changelog) lists each one.</Callout>

## What it looks like

**On your phone, in Claude.** You're reading about a hotel for a trip. You ask Claude to save the page and the PDF of its price list to Marfa, tagged `lisbon`. Back at your Mac, Claude Code finds both when you ask what you saved for the trip.

**In your editor.** Your notes are a folder of Markdown files on your Mac, kept in step with Marfa. You write in any editor you like, and so can an agent. Drop a photo in and embed it in a note, and the photo is kept in Marfa as a file of its own, beside the note that shows it.

**From another service.** The Todoist connector brings your tasks into your feed in Marfa. Ask your agent which tasks are due this week, or have it mark one done, and the connector carries the change back to Todoist.

## How it fits together

One running Marfa is an **instance**. It belongs to one person, its owner, and holds their data in the middle. Everything else reaches it by one of five routes, and connectors join it to services you already use.

<MarfaAtAGlance />

Two routes **keep a copy on the device**, so they work offline and send their changes when they reach the instance again. A [synced folder](/use/sync-a-folder) is one: each note is a Markdown file that you, or an agent, edit in any app. An iPhone or Mac app built on the [Swift SDK](/swift-sdk) is the other.

Three routes **call the instance directly**. Claude, ChatGPT, Claude Code and Codex connect to its [MCP server](/marfa-mcp-server). The [CLI](/cli), the `marfa` program, does everything from a terminal on any computer, and a script you write calls the [HTTP API](/api-reference).

[Connectors](/use/connect-a-service) copy records from services such as Todoist and GitHub, and some carry your changes back. Every route and connector uses a credential that you can [see and revoke](/use/see-and-cut-access) at any time.

If you know Apple's platforms, Marfa is **like iCloud, but open to any app**, and **like HealthKit, but for any kind of data**: each app asks for the kinds of things it needs and sees nothing else.

## What Marfa isn't

- **Not for teams.** Other people don't share or collaborate in your instance.
- **Not hosted for you.** Your data lives on the machine you run Marfa on, and in any storage you choose to attach.
- **Not an app.** You don't need any particular app. Apps are built on Marfa, and anyone can build one.
- **Not a place to run code.** Apps, agents and connectors run outside Marfa and reach it over its API.

## Where it runs

Marfa runs on your own Mac, a Linux machine, or Railway, with no database server to set up. Your devices reach it on your own network, from anywhere over a private network such as Tailscale, or at a public address on your own domain, which Claude and ChatGPT need. [Give your instance an address](/run/address) covers each.

Marfa is open source under the Apache License 2.0. The rules it follows are written down in [the contract](https://github.com/withmarfa/marfa/tree/main/conformance/spec) and checked by tests.
