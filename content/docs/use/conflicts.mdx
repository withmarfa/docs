A note can change in two places at once, such as in your editor and from an agent, or in two directories that follow one folder. **Most edits merge:** a tag added in one place and a new body in the other both reach the note. When two edits rewrite the same text, Marfa keeps both, and one becomes a conflicted copy.

## How a copy appears

Only a note's text makes a copy: its body, or its `notes` property. **The edit that reaches your instance first keeps the note,** and the other becomes a new note beside it, in every directory that follows the folder. The sync says so:

```text
conflicted update_item Packing.md conflicted copy Packing (2).md merged body
```

The copy holds the second edit, word for word, under the same title, so its file gets a number:

```markdown title="Home/Packing (2).md"
---
type: core.note
tier: library
tags:
  - conflicted-copy
title: Packing
derived-from:
  - "[[01a1f445-ee0e-737b-97d9-eb271f922019]]"
marfa_id: 0000ff92-403e-75de-8fdf-e694ade1fb24
marfa_version: 1
---
Passport, sun hat, sandals.
```

- **It's tagged `conflicted-copy`,** so `marfa items list --tag conflicted-copy` finds every one.
- **Its `derived-from` line** names the original note.
- **It's a note of its own,** so nothing you do to it changes the original.

## Keep the version you want

1. Open both files, `Packing.md` and `Packing (2).md`.
2. Copy what you want to keep from the copy into the original, and save it.
3. Delete the copy's file. It moves to the bin, where you can restore it.

**Edit the original, not the copy,** so the note's links, tags and history stay with it. An agent can do the same: ask it to find notes tagged `conflicted-copy` and merge each pair as you say.

To make copies rare, keep a [watch](/use/keep-folders-syncing) running on every computer, so each edit reaches the others before the next one starts. [Versions](/build/versions) explains how Marfa decides what merges.
