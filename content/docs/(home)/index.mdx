Marfa is one home, on a computer or server of your own, for everything you keep: **your files and pictures first, then your notes and bookmarks, and what you keep in other services.** Every app and agent you approve works from that one copy and reaches only what you allow, so **you keep your data when you change apps.**

<Callout type="warn" title="Marfa is early: expect breaking changes until version 1.0">The [Changelog](/changelog) lists each one.</Callout>

## What it looks like

**On your phone, in Claude.** You're reading about a hotel for a trip. You ask Claude to save the page and the PDF of its price list to Marfa, tagged `lisbon`. Back at your Mac, Claude Code finds both when you ask what you saved for the trip.

**In a folder.** Your files and pictures, and your notes as Markdown, sit in a folder on your computer, kept in step with Marfa. You open them in any app, and so can an agent.

**Across your agents.** Codex saves the receipts and contracts it finds in a project to Marfa. Later you ask Claude which are due for renewal, and it reads the same items. An agent that summarizes your reading holds a key that sees nothing else.

## How it fits together

One running Marfa is an **instance.** It belongs to one person, its owner, and keeps the one copy of their data that everything else works from:

<MarfaAtAGlance />

An agent, a folder, an app or a script reaches it with a credential that you can [see and revoke](/use/see-and-revoke-access) at any time. [How Marfa works](/how-marfa-works) explains the few ideas behind it.

## What Marfa isn't

- **Not for teams.** Other people don't share or collaborate in your instance.
- **Not hosted for you.** Your data lives on the machine you run Marfa on, and in any storage you attach.
- **Not an app.** You see your things through an agent, the CLI or a synced folder. The instance's own pages in your browser are only for setting up, signing in and approving access, and never show them.
- **Not a place to run code.** Apps, agents and connectors run outside Marfa and reach it over its API.

## Where it runs

Marfa runs on a Mac with Apple silicon, a Linux machine, or Railway, with no database server to set up. Your devices reach it on your own network, over a private network such as Tailscale, or at a public address, which Claude and ChatGPT need. [Give your instance an address](/run/address) covers each. Marfa is open source under the Apache License 2.0.
